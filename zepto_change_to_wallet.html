01
The Problem

30–40% of Zepto orders are COD. Every one has a change problem.

Cash on Delivery isn't going anywhere in India. But the current experience at the doorstep introduces three friction points that compound at scale:

30-40%
of Zepto orders in India are paid cash on delivery
30s
average wait when the DA doesn't have exact change
₹0
digital record of cash exchanged today — disputes have no proof

Delivery Agents carry change for every order. At quick-commerce scale, managing float is operationally complex and a personal working-capital burden absorbed by riders.

Customers rarely have exact change. The resulting doorstep fumble adds friction to the last mile, creates failed delivery attempts, and leaves a poor final impression of an otherwise seamless experience.

No digital record exists. When a customer later claims they paid ₹1,000 instead of ₹500, there is no proof on either side. Disputes become he-said-she-said with no resolution path.

02
The Thesis

Cash was always the last friction in quick commerce.

The idea in one breath
If you pay cash and the rider can't make change, the difference lands in your Zepto Cash instantly. That balance pays for your next order — or reduces the cash you hand over next time. Cash becomes a recurring digital deposit.

Since Zepto Cash already exists for every user, there's no wallet setup, no onboarding, no RBI PPI license required (it's a closed-loop instrument). The entire infrastructure exists. Only the bridge is missing.

03
The Solution

A four-step flow that makes change disappear.

i.
Customer opts in at checkout

Selecting COD surfaces a toggle: "Change to Zepto Cash — receive any change instantly as wallet credit." Disclaimer shown. Feature is opt-in, never forced. Wallet already exists for every Zepto user — no setup step.

ii.
DA enters cash received at doorstep

The partner app shows a wallet-flag indicator on the order card. On completion, DA enters the note denomination received (₹500). System auto-calculates change: ₹500 − ₹389 = ₹111.

iii.
Customer confirms in real time

Push notification fires within 5 seconds: "₹500 recorded by your rider. ₹111 will be credited to Zepto Cash — Confirm or Dispute." Customer has 30 seconds. This is the proof layer — both sides get a digital record, timestamped.

iv.
₹111 credited. Rider keeps ₹500. Done.

After DA double-confirmation and customer confirm, the credit fires instantly to Zepto Cash. The cash amount is immutably logged against the order ID for day-end deposit reconciliation. Rider's deposit obligation auto-calculates: ₹500 − ₹111 = ₹389.

05
The Partial-Cash Innovation

Wallet balance reduces cash needed. Every time.

The most powerful sub-feature: when a customer has any Zepto Cash balance, the checkout auto-applies it against the COD amount. The customer hands over a smaller, cleaner number — and if change comes back, it loops straight into the wallet again.

This creates a self-reinforcing loop: COD change goes to wallet → wallet reduces next COD → less change needed → fewer friction moments at the door. The product experience gets better every order.

06
Dispute Handling

Why we rejected the obvious solution.

The first instinct for dispute prevention is to have the DA photograph the cash handed over. We rejected it — and chose something better.

Rejected

Photo of cash at doorstep

Adds 5–10 seconds of friction (defeats the purpose), creates per-order storage cost, raises privacy concerns, and a photo of a note doesn't prove what was handed over. You could photograph any note.

Selected

Real-time customer dual-confirmation

Mirrors how UPI already works. When the DA enters ₹500 in the partner app, the customer's phone gets a push within 5 seconds:

"₹500 cash recorded by your delivery partner. Change of ₹111 will be credited to Zepto Cash. Confirm or Dispute."
→
Customer taps Confirm

Wallet credit fires instantly. Both sides have a timestamped digital record. Dispute-proof by design.

✕
Customer taps Dispute

Wallet credit does not happen. Physical change flow kicks in. DA hands change as today. No credit issued.

—
No response in 30 seconds

Auto-confirm. Wallet credit fires. Customer opted into this flow at checkout — no-response is consent-by-default.

⚠
2+ disputes in 30 days

Customer is auto-opted out. Feature paused for them — defaults to physical change. Prevents repeat fraud claims without blocking good-faith disputes.

07
Guardrails

Cash-to-digital limits that keep the system honest.

Converting cash to digital credit needs hard boundaries. These numbers aren't arbitrary — each one is sized to the real use-case while closing obvious abuse vectors.

₹500
Per-order cap
Max change credited from any single COD order. Covers 99% of real cases. Above this, physical change fallback.
₹1,000
Per-day cap
Total wallet credits per user per day. Prevents abuse via back-to-back COD orders with inflated entries.
₹2,000
Wallet ceiling
Max Zepto Cash from change credits. Forces money to flow back through Zepto — prevents the wallet becoming dormant savings.
30s
Dispute window
Customer confirms or disputes via push before credit fires. UPI-style real-time proof, zero photo overhead.
<1%
DA error threshold
DA cash-entry error rate above 1% in 2 weeks auto-pauses their feature access and flags to Ops for retraining.
∞
Idempotency
Credit API keyed on order_id. Network retry, double-tap, or duplicate confirm cannot issue a double credit.

10
Strategic Rationale

Why this is the right bet for Zepto right now.

i.
Zepto Cash is already live

No new wallet to onboard, no RBI PPI license required. The infrastructure exists. Only the bridge from COD to wallet is missing.

ii.
COD is 30–40% of all orders

At 30% opt-in on 30% COD orders — conservatively 10M+ change events per year. Each one seeds the next order's payment habit.

iii.
Competition hasn't shipped it

Blinkit, Instamart, Swiggy — none have wired COD change to wallet. First mover owns the doorstep payment narrative.

iv.
Closed-loop wallet adoption

Money spent from Zepto Cash bypasses external gateway costs. Every wallet order is a direct margin improvement, compounding per order.

v.
Habit-forming retention loop

A wallet with money in it is a reason to order again. Each COD seeds the next order's payment — without the customer doing anything.

vi.
DA experience improvement

Riders who opt-in customers reduce the float they need to carry. Fewer change transactions = fewer errors, faster deliveries, less end-of-day reconciliation stress.
